                                    Situation Overview and Humanitarian Needs

                                    Hostilities in Lebanon continue to have a devastating impact on civilians. As of 1 April, at least 1,318 people have reportedly
                                    been killed, including 125 children, and 3,935 injured, including 429 children, according to the Ministry of Public Health
                                    (MoPH).

                                    Mass displacement is ongoing amid intensified strikes, repeated displacement orders, and the expansion of affected areas.
                                    As of 1 April, over 1.1 million people1 are displaced, including more than 390,000 children, with 136,321 individuals sheltering
                                    in 669 official collective shelters.2 Continued strikes in densely populated urban areas, including Beirut's southern suburbs,
                                    risk triggering further large-scale displacement and compounding already overstretched shelter capacity.3

                                    Cross-border movements into Syria continue to rise; between 2 and 27 March, over 202,477 individuals crossed from
                                    Lebanon into Syria through official border points, including 175,134 Syrians and 27,343 Lebanese nationals.4

Hostilities remain widespread across Beirut's southern suburbs, southern Lebanon, and the Bekaa, with continued artillery
fire and reported ground incursions along the Blue Line.5 Civilian infrastructure continues to be impacted, including 17
reported incidents affecting water systems across Baalbek-Hermel, Bekaa, South, and Nabatieh, damaging reservoirs,
pumping stations, solar systems, and main supply lines, and disrupting access to safe water.

Attacks on healthcare and emergency services remain a major concern. Since 2 March, 53 healthcare workers have been
killed and 137 injured, alongside 82 reported attacks on emergency medical services. A total of 67 vehicles and 20 medical
facilities have been impacted, and five hospitals have been forced to close. 6 Overcrowded shelter conditions are contributing
to the emergence of communicable diseases, including scabies, lice, and varicella, increasing public health risks. Access to
health services remains constrained, particularly for populations in hard-to-reach areas with limited mobility. These
challenges are compounded by the impact of ongoing hostilities on healthcare facilities, further reducing the availability and
accessibility of essential services.

Education in Lebanon has been severely disrupted by escalating hostilities and mass displacement. Of the 669 collective
shelters, 364 are public schools and 58 are Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) institutions, greatly
reducing available learning spaces. In addition, 439 public schools across South and Nabatieh, Bekaa, Baalbek, and Beirut's
southern suburbs remain closed due to insecurity, evacuation orders, and proximity to conflict zones. These closures have
directly affected approximately 256,000 students, including 181,500 in the morning shift and 74,500 in the afternoon shift.
The conversion of 58 TVET schools into shelters has disrupted access for 16,400 youth, while the closure of 46 additional
TVET schools in South Lebanon and Bekaa has affected a further 16,850 youth. Following three weeks of school closures, the
Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE) has initiated a gradual reopening of schools through blended learning
modalities. Around 300 schools have resumed learning in safer locations, primarily in North Lebanon and Akkar, including 50
designated hub schools operating double-shift systems to accommodate students from closed or shelter schools. Further
reopenings in Mount Lebanon and Bekaa are anticipated. At the TVET level, 55 public schools have resumed in-person
learning in safe areas, while 103 schools, either closed or currently used as shelters, have transitioned to online learning
delivered by TVET teachers. However, despite these efforts, access to education remains uneven, particularly for displaced
children and youth facing barriers such as limited connectivity, lack of safe learning spaces, and ongoing insecurity,
constraining continuity of learning. Prolonged disruption to education risks increased dropout rates and negative coping
mechanisms among children and adolescents, particularly among displaced populations.

According to a recent rapid gender analysis by UN Women, protection risks in Lebanon are increasingly driven by the
conditions of displacement. Overcrowding, lack of privacy, insecure living arrangements, and dependence on others for
shelter are heightening exposure to gender-based violence, exploitation, and domestic tension. At the same time, mobility
restrictions, information gaps, and documentation barriers are limiting access to humanitarian assistance,
disproportionately affecting women, particularly those with caregiving responsibilities. Displacement is also increasing
unpaid care burdens and reducing access to income and resources, while households adopt harmful coping strategies,
further straining their capacity. Risks are particularly acute for female-headed households, adolescent girls, older women,
persons with disabilities, and migrant and refugee women. These dynamics underscore the need for targeted, gender-
responsive interventions to ensure safe and equitable access to services.7

Summary Analysis of Programme Response

Water, sanitation and hygiene

In coordination with the Ministry of Energy and Water (MoEW), UNICEF supplied over 280,000 litres of fuel to Water
Establishments, supporting the operation of approximately 45 water facilities, including water pumping stations, and 19
wastewater stations nationwide. In parallel, UNICEF undertook critical repair and maintenance works, including 42 repairs
to water systems, primarily water pumping stations and associated networks, and six repairs to wastewater systems. These
fuel, repair, and maintenance interventions directly supported the continuity of public water supply and wastewater services
for more than 2,650,000 people, including an estimated 385,000 IDPs hosted within communities relying on these systems.

UNICEF partners are delivering emergency WASH assistance in 216 collective shelters across Lebanon, reaching an
estimated 42,822 IDPs, alongside the distribution of essential hygiene items through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM).
Support in collective shelters includes repairs to existing WASH facilities and the installation of new showers and latrines,

Rapid Response Mechanism

UNICEF's RRM has been activated since 2 March 2026 to address displacement waves and reach families on the move, in
shelters, and those remaining in hard-to-reach areas, serving as an agile, multi sectoral response platform linked to WASH,
nutrition, protection, and other emergency interventions.

As of 1 April, UNICEF's RRM has reached 188,155 IDPs in more than 360 shelters, people on the move, and in hard-to-reach
areas with essential non-food items (NFIs). Distributions included 446,319 bottles of water (1.5L each), 30,823 family hygiene
kits, 15,416 baby kits, 19,188 dignity kits, 1,882 packs of adult diapers and sanitary pads, and 1,330 boxes of high-energy
biscuits for children aged six months and above and pregnant women. Winterization support included 28,466 blankets and
21,301 children's clothing kits. In addition, around 4,700 families on the move in Beirut, Saida, and Tyre were supported with
light RRM kits and bottled water.

As part of the RRM response, joint WFP­UNICEF convoys continued to deliver life-saving supplies to children and families in
hard-to-reach areas, despite severe access constraints and ongoing hostilities. Convoys reached approximately 27,000­
28,000 people, including 9,000­10,000 children, across Tyre, Rmeich/Ein Ebel/Debel, and Qlaya'a­Marjaayoun, delivering
safe water, hygiene kits, family hygiene kits, dignity kits, and baby kits. UNICEF continues to prioritise access to hard-to-reach
areas, sustaining delivery of life-saving support while assessing gaps to inform a more continuous, child-centred response.
In parallel, a UNICEF-led mission to Nabatieh on 31 March delivered 2,200 hygiene kits to support 4,075 families, including
IDPs, across 26 villages in the Al Sheqif Union of Municipalities. This intervention targeted both displaced and host
communities living in areas witnessing active fighting, ensuring coverage across collective centres and villages hosting IDPs.

Child protection and GBViE

Community-Based Psychosocial Support (CBPSS) reached 8,675 children and caregivers through in-person sessions in
shelters and community spaces, combining structured support with recreational activities to promote well-being, resilience,
and positive social interaction. Caregivers were also supported with practical guidance on stress management,
communication with children, protection risks, and available services and referral pathways.

A total of 26 children at risk or survivors of violence received case management services. In parallel, 21 unaccompanied and
separated children were identified, of whom 20 were reunified or placed in community-based care. Awareness materials on
child separation during conflict have been disseminated across 40 public and private hospitals nationwide.

Child protection and psychosocial support messaging reached 16,167 individuals through direct outreach and social media,
promoting safer practices and strengthening families' ability to support children. The digital explosive ordnance risk
education (EORE) campaign, launched across multiple platforms, has reached over one million individuals nationwide,
including in hard-to-reach areas. In addition, 473 frontline workers were reached through online sensitization sessions, and
31,966 EORE IEC materials have been distributed in shelters across the South, West Bekaa, and Baalbek-Hermel.

Efforts to mitigate heightened gender-based violence (GBV) risks continue to be scaled up, including awareness sessions on
GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), psychosocial activities, and GBV safety audits in collective shelters. Dignity
and LAHA kits have also been distributed in shelters. PSEA materials, including reporting procedures and hotlines, have been
displayed across shelters, while POWER4Girls emergency sessions continue to provide adolescent girls with life-saving
information and practical skills. Despite these efforts, identification of GBV survivors remains limited due to overcrowding,
lack of privacy, and non-conducive conditions in collective shelters, which constrain safe disclosure. Ongoing awareness-
raising and outreach aim to improve reporting and access to services.

Education

UNICEF continues to support MEHE in rolling out a phased response to ensure continuity of learning for children affected by
the crisis. After more than three weeks of school closures, MEHE has begun gradual reopening in safe areas, combining in-
person and online learning. Around 300 schools, mainly in North Lebanon and Akkar, have resumed learning, supporting
116,800 students through the Transition and Resilience Education Fund (TREF). This includes 50 hub schools hosting
students from other closed or shelter schools on alternating schedules. Further reopenings are expected in Mount Lebanon
and Bekaa.

UNICEF is also supporting universal remote learning for all public school students through multiple digital platforms.
Accounts and virtual classrooms have been established, reaching 40,000 teachers and 390,000 students. Collaboration with
the Ministry of Telecommunications provides up to 20GB of free data per weekday, while monitoring mechanisms on MS
Teams and Madristi track participation. In the first two weeks, 56,000 students and 12,400 teachers logged in, highlighting
uptake of remote modalities.

Through the Multiple Flexible Pathways (MFP) programme, 607 education kits have been distributed in 104 collective shelters,
reaching 30,350 children with items such as learning supplies, recreational supplies, and LEGO playboxes. In addition,
26,227 children are accessing integrated education services across 32 Makani centres and 33 Dirasa schools in safe
locations. Community outreach efforts by implementing partners have enabled 900 displaced children from nearby shelters
and host communities, particularly in Beirut and Mount Lebanon, to participate in Makani-based learning and integrated
support activities, with continued work to scale up capacity in all existing and open Makani centres and Dirasa schools in
proximity to shelters and catchment areas of displaced children, as well as introducing additional shifts and a rotational
schedule to maximise access to learning and integrated services for crisis affected children.

The work to establish Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) to support displaced and crisis-affected students is ongoing. To date,
30 out of the 75 targeted TLS have signed agreements to host IDP students across Bekaa, Baalbek, Saida, Mount Lebanon,
and Beirut, with a phased rollout of activities underway. These TLS will deliver a range of flexible, context-adapted education
modalities, including Retention Support (RS) for in-school children and Accelerated Learning Programme for out-of-school
children, complemented by psychosocial support, recreational, and sports activities. In addition to in-person learning, the
approach will integrate digital and hybrid learning options, including the use of digital hubs, to ensure continuity of learning
for displaced children where access to physical spaces remains limited.

The Inclusive Education Programme is mobilizing MEHE multidisciplinary teams to support children with disabilities during
the current crisis. To ensure seamless support across online, blended, and in-person modalities, approximately 315
paraprofessionals and special educators training were trained on the Microsoft Teams and Madristi platforms on 26 and 27
March. Updated terms of reference were issued last week to reflect the specific roles and responsibilities in emergency
learning contexts. Support for children with disabilities is also being integrated into MFP activities, including the provision of
assistive devices, therapy, and referrals. In shelters, to date, MFP partners have identified 273 children with disabilities and
204 adults with disabilities. Of these, 45 children with disabilities have received assistive devices, 45 have started therapy
services, and a total of 52 children with disabilities have been supported so far. In addition, 75 adults with disabilities have
been referred to other service providers. This multi-pronged approach ensures continuity of education, inclusion, and
protection for all learners, particularly displaced children and those with disabilities.

Health

Since the onset of the emergency, UNICEF has supported the health response for displaced populations through the delivery
of more than 140 tons of essential medications and medical supplies to MoPH Primary Healthcare Satellite Units (PSUs) and
governmental hospitals. UNICEF has supported the MoPH to activate mobile health services through its PSU and AaSalameh
interventions, delivering primary healthcare services to displaced populations. These clinics cover up to 30 per cent of the
total shelters covered by the MoPH, providing essential primary healthcare services. In addition to internally displaced
persons (IDPs) residing in shelters, UNICEF-supported units cover host communities and hardtoreach areas. Currently, 18
PSUs and 16 AaSalameh centres remain operational in hardtoreach locations. To date, PSUs (mobile clinics) have delivered
more than 23,717 primary healthcare services, with 10,627 beneficiaries receiving essential medications.

Health surveillance in shelters indicates that the most common conditions identified are communicable conditions,
particularly lice and scabies, linked to overcrowding and living conditions. In response, UNICEF has supported the MoPH with
large quantities of treatment supplies for both conditions, which have been directly distributed to all PSUs and AaSalameh

Through the AaSalameh primary healthcare community outreach programme, 5,006 children were vaccinated to date.
UNICEF also continues to sustain support to the MoPH Public Health Emergency Operations Centre (PHEOC) to ensure

caregivers received mental health and psychosocial support.

Nutrition

UNICEF has identified a critical gap in the availability of breastmilk substitute (BMS) kits for non-breastfed infants aged 0­12
months residing in shelters. These kits are essential to ensure the safe preparation and use of infant formula when
breastfeeding is not possible and only following appropriate assessment. In response, UNICEF, in its capacity as Nutrition
Sector lead, has advocated for the prioritization of BMS kits among partners and issued a Letter of Invitation to Bid for the
procurement of 3,000 kits.

A total of 2,080 caregivers of children under five received integrated Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF), nutrition, and Early
Childhood Development (ECD) counselling. Of these, 375 caregivers accessed guidance on optimal infant feeding practices
in emergencies through the IYCF chatbot, while 207 caregivers contacted the national IYCF hotline, with 134 referred for
specialized support. In parallel, IYCF rapid assessments reached 96 caregivers to identify and refer children requiring
specialized services.

UNICEF continues to integrate essential nutrition and ECD interventions across multiple service delivery platforms, including
Primary Health Care Centres (PHCCs), PSUs, nutrition partners, and education partners, to support internally displaced and
vulnerable populations. To date, 40 PSUs have been supported with nutritionists, extending coverage to more than 99
shelters. Of these, 37 PSUs are actively delivering nutrition services across 87 shelters, while three additional PSUs have
recently become operational following recruitment and capacity building on nutrition and wasting management.

Eight education partners have been mobilized to deliver ECD-in-emergencies services across more than 53 collective
shelters. Integrated nutrition and ECD services also continue through Makani centres in host communities, ensuring
continuity of care and expanded coverage beyond shelter settings.

To prevent wasting among high-risk groups, 240,500 high-energy biscuits (HEBs), 167,412 ready-to-use complementary food
(RUCF) jars, and other energy-protein supplements have been prepositioned, sufficient to support over 30,074 children and
pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW) for one month.

To date, 9,804 children under five have received emergency food rations and micronutrient supplementation sufficient for
one month, including HEBs, small-quantity lipid-based nutrient supplements (SQLNS), and complementary food jars. In
addition, 624 pregnant and breastfeeding women and adolescent girls received micronutrient supplementation to prevent
deficiencies. Screening efforts reached 4,166 children under five and 534 PBW, identifying 92 children and 25 women with
wasting, all of whom were referred for treatment.

Social Protection and Humanitarian Cash Assistance

As part of the immediate response to the current escalation and to support beneficiaries of the National Disability Allowance
(NDA), UNICEF and the Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA) advanced the regular cash assistance payment for 38,000 Lebanese
and non-Lebanese NDA beneficiaries nationwide, beginning on 10 March.

Building on previous emergency cash responses in 2024, UNICEF, MoSA, and the ILO have activated emergency financial
support for families with persons with disabilities affected by the conflict. This shock-responsive expansion of the NDA has
provided urgent cash assistance to offset additional costs related to disability during displacement, including access to
healthcare, assistive services, and basic needs. Using established NDA delivery systems and registries, the first phase of this
intervention was launched on 11 March and has reached approximately 8,000 families (6,000 Lebanese and 2,000 non-

Lebanese, or around 33,000 individuals) as of 19 March, with each household receiving a US$100 grant for households with
a child or young person aged 0­19 years living with a disability.

A second phase of the shock-responsive expansion of the NDA has been initiated, through an additional allocation of
resources. In this second phase, an additional 4,000 families (3,000 Lebanese and 100 non-Lebanese, or around 16,000
individuals) with a young person aged 20­27 years living with a disability will be receiving, starting 2 April, a US$ 100

The overall target for this emergency support is 45,000 families with a person with a disability from affected areas, to be
reached in a phased approach over three months through a one-off cash transfer based on the availability of resources. This
programme complements the national Shock-Responsive Social Protection system, also led by the government, ensuring
continuity of support and mitigating the immediate financial impact of displacement on vulnerable households.

Youth and Adolescent Development

UNICEF continues to engage youth as active contributors to emergency response efforts, supporting both service delivery
and community resilience.

To ensure continuity of formal TVET education, UNICEF provided technical support to the TVET Directorate to finalize and
implement its learning continuity plan, in line with the Government's directive to resume learning from 30 March 2026. The
plan includes both online and in-person modalities, targeting 51,600 TVET students and youth across Lebanon. To date, 55
public TVET schools have progressively resumed in-person learning in safe areas, while 103 schools, either closed or
currently serving as shelters, have transitioned to online learning delivered by TVET teachers. UNICEF is also supporting the
identification of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) to enable online and blended learning for displaced youth. Digital TVET
modules are being prepared for rollout in selected shelters, with additional modules under development to further expand
access.

In parallel, 15 TVET schools currently functioning as shelters and hosting 8,161 IDPs have received in-kind support from
UNICEF, valued at US$ 50,000, to cover urgent needs including basic supplies, fuel, and essential materials. Non-Formal
skilling programmes continue in non-affected areas, reaching 610 youth, while delivery in affected locations has shifted to
online modalities where feasible.

Youth are also directly contributing to emergency response efforts through practical service delivery. Youth-produced items
have been distributed across 26 TVET shelters, including 682 sleeping bags, 500 blankets, 200 Eid clothing kits, and 3,119
winter kits. Under work-based learning and income-generation initiatives, 41 youth supported food security efforts by
producing 19,871 hot meals in coordination with WFP across three TVET shelter kitchens, while 71 youth in Baalbek produced
and distributed 4,106 cold meals across 26 shelters. An additional 22 youth have initiated sleeping bag production, with 300
completed to date. Six youth barbers have also contributed to hygiene support, providing haircuts to 65 IDPs in two shelters.

Youth engagement in social cohesion and psychosocial support activities is ongoing, with 21 trained youth facilitators
delivering sessions and the adolescent kit, reaching 461 children and adolescents across six TVET shelters.

Risk Communication and Community Engagement, Accountability to Affected
Populations, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Gender, and Inclusion

Between 26 and 31 March, UNICEF call centres received 1,344 calls from IDPs and host communities. Inquiries primarily
related to shelter access for non-Lebanese populations, emergency cash assistance, non-food items (NFIs), access to newly
introduced online learning services, and WASH gaps in informal settlements in Akkar. All cases were followed up through
referrals and appropriate actions to ensure timely response to community needs.

To better understand gaps among the most vulnerable, UNICEF partnered with the women-led organization FEMALE to
conduct rapid assessments of families living in open spaces in Beirut and Saida. To date, 240 households have been
assessed, with an average household size of 4.8 persons. Findings indicate that many households are unable to access
shelters due to overcrowding, safety concerns, access restrictions, and transportation barriers. Critical gaps include limited
access to sanitation, lack of privacy, protection risks, and lack of access to education for children. Referrals for high-risk
cases are ongoing, particularly for pregnant and lactating women, young children, persons with disabilities, and separated
children. UNICEF is responding through targeted interventions, including mobile medical units, preparation of tailored
education responses, and advocacy to address access barriers.

Multisectoral services, including Makani programming, are being delivered through operational centres across camps and

Child protection and psychosocial support remain central to the response. To date, 5,291 children have accessed non -

Preventive and awareness-raising activities reached 5,099 individuals through child protection and children's rights sessions,

support for children aged 6­14, alongside early childhood education activities for children aged 3­5, to ensure age-
appropriate learning opportunities and continuity of education during the crisis.

Supply and Logistics

Since the onset of the emergency, UNICEF has mobilized US$ 4.9 million in procurement to support its multi-sectoral
response in Lebanon. An airbridge between Copenhagen and Beirut, established with support from UNICEF Supply Division
and partners including ECHO, the EU Humanitarian Air Bridge (EU HAB), and DSV Logistics, has enabled the rapid delivery of
critical supplies. To date, two charter flights carrying 72 metric tons of medical and emergency supplies have arrived, with a
third shipment of 20 metric tons currently in preparation.

In parallel, US$4.8 million worth of prepositioned supplies, equivalent to 1,100 metric tons, have been dispatched during the
first month of the response. This includes US$1.9 million in emergency supplies, comprising 71,790 emergency kits, 16,470
winter clothing kits, and 420,000 bottles of water, as well as US$ 2 million in medical and nutrition supplies, in addition to
education and WASH materials. Distributions have been implemented through government and local partners, including the
MoPH, MEHE, MoSA, and civil society organizations. Funding is urgently needed to replenish essential supplies for continued
distribution to the most vulnerable populations.

In hard-to-reach areas, distributions continue through coordinated convoys. The replenishment of emergency stocks is
underway through local procurement, with 58,431 kits, including baby kits, hygiene kits, and dignity kits, currently in
production.

Additional service providers have been mobilized to support critical infrastructure and service continuity, including the
installation of latrines and showers in shelters, fuel delivery to water establishments, and maintenance of water systems
nationwide. Printing of emergency learning materials for 50,000 children is also ongoing.

To enhance operational safety and access, an additional armoured vehicle has been procured to support staff deployment
to hard-to-reach and high-risk areas.

UNICEF is also supporting the MoPH logistics operations at the Karantina Central Medical Store, with the deployment of a
logistician contributing to warehouse and inventory management, as well as emergency distributions. Additional manpower
and transport capacity are being provided to reinforce day-to-day operations.

Humanitarian Leadership, Coordination and Strategy

On 28 February 2026, MoSA, designated by the Government as the sole authority to coordinate with the Humanitarian Country
Team (HCT) in the event of displacement, convened a meeting with Heads of UN Agencies to align on preparedness measures
in anticipation of a potential escalation. During the meeting, MoSA confirmed that the agreed coordination architecture falls
under the Lebanese Response Plan (LRP), through the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) framework, led jointly by MoSA
and the Ministry of Interior and Municipalities (MoIM). At the sub-national level, coordination is led by Governors in
coordination with the National Disaster Risk Management Unit.

Within this framework, UNICEF co-leads the Education sector and leads the WASH and Nutrition sectors, as well as the Child
Protection Working Group, at both national and sub-national levels. UNICEF also contributes to the Health, GBV and AAP,
Gender, Livelihoods, and Basic Assistance sectors. UNICEF is also the co-lead of the Assessment and Analysis Working
Group. At the sub-national level, the Operational Coordination Group (OCG) is coordinating the response in close
collaboration with MoSA and Governors. UNICEF-led sectors are continuously monitoring partners' emergency response
capacities and contingency stock levels, while ensuring that sector-specific preparedness actions are undertaken in
coordination with partners.

UNICEF is delivering humanitarian programming for people in need across Lebanon in collective shelters and communities
hosting IDPs; in hot spot areas such as directly conflict affected and hard-to-reach areas South of the Litani River and in the
Bekaa; and in the border areas where people are seeking to leave Lebanon. This humanitarian response will continue to reach
all vulnerable population groups including Lebanese, Syrians and Palestine refugees, including through leveraging funding
from the LRP. UNICEF will deliver programmes that reach broad catchment populations such as operations and maintenance
of the water systems and primary health care, while also supporting targeted interventions such as cash transfers to families
and individuals and youth engagement.

Funding Overview and Partnerships

UNICEF's threemonth Preparedness and Response Plan requires US$ 48.3 million to reach 1 million people in need. There
is US$ 10.9 million in available funds for this response, in addition to an internal Emergency Programme Fund (EPF) loan of
US$ 6 million, an internal UNICEF financial mechanism that helps ensure readiness and rapid response during emergencies
which will need to be reimbursed once funding is received. Despite this support, a 77 per cent funding gap remains. Without
additional resources, UNICEF will be unable to maintain critical services or respond to the escalating needs of affected
children and their families.
